---
build123d: minor
---

Mesh build123d models in millimetres and degrees, with the same tessellation presets as replicad. Before, build123d passed OCCT's relative flag, so a `linearTolerance` labelled millimetres was a ratio of each edge's size and `angularTolerance` was radians. Typing 0.05 into build123d and into replicad gave meshes two orders of magnitude apart.

A build123d render or GLB export now takes `occtTessellationSchema` from `@taucad/occt-core`: a preset (`low`, `medium`, `high`, `extra-high`) or an explicit `{ linearTolerance?, angularTolerance? }` in millimetres and degrees, never both. A preset is derived from the bounding-box diagonal of the model's shapes, which the Python worker measures only when a preset is requested. OCCT then meshes in absolute mode. Renders default to `medium` and GLB exports to `high`. A render over 2,000,000 triangles is re-meshed one step coarser, at most three times, and warns `TESSELLATION_DOWNGRADED`. A GLB export over 20,000,000 triangles fails with `RESOURCE_LIMIT` and a suggested tolerance; it is never coarsened. Each render reports `tessellation` with the requested form, the tolerances it meshed at, the steps it coarsened, its triangle and vertex counts and the time it took. Spheres of radius 5 mm and 500 mm both mesh to 5,860 triangles at `medium`, the same count replicad produces.

Stored values: kernel settings saved before this release keep their keys, `linearTolerance` and `angularTolerance`, but hold a ratio and an angle in radians of at most π. build123d reads an explicit `angularTolerance` at or below π as such a value. It meshes at the lane's default preset (`medium` for renders, `high` for GLB exports) and warns `VIEW_OPTIONS_INVALID` or `EXPORT_OPTIONS_INVALID` with the stale value, so a saved 0.05 never becomes 0.05 mm without notice. Saving the setting again, as a preset or as explicit tolerances with an angle above π degrees, clears the warning. The kernel version gains `tessellation1`, so cached meshes regenerate.
