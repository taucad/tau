The canvas actor presents the view's camera on its surface: a resize reaches the camera as `setViewport` in the macrostep the canvas reports it, before the frame renders, and the camera's clip policy pads the far plane for the infinite grid while the grid is in the canvas's scene, across the axis the grid lies on (the scene reports both with the new `gridChanged` canvas event), and clears when the canvas is lost, fails or stops. The `upDirection` prop of `ThreeCanvasInstance`, `ThreeProvider` and `CadViewer` is no longer read: the grid clip follows graphics' up direction, the axis the grid is drawn on. The controls adapter steps `camera-controls` each frame. Remove `TauCameraControls` and the `graphics/three/controls/tau-camera-controls` subpath: import `resolveCameraControlProps` and `resolveCameraControlMouseButtons` from `graphics/three/controls/camera-controls-feel`, which also exports their `CameraControlsFeel` return type (the `camera-controls` properties they set) in place of `TauCameraControls`' props. `retargetCameraControls` and `shouldUpdateTauCameraControlsFrame` are no longer exported from the package. The canvas machine exports `resolveGridClipPolicy`, `CanvasGrid` and `CanvasCameraRef`, its input takes the view's `camera`, and `CanvasContext` gains `cameraSurface`. `ViewportGizmoInteractionLockProvider` takes an optional `lock` to provide, and a provider nested inside another now shares the enclosing lock instead of making its own; the controls adapter does not step the controls while the canvas's lock is held.
