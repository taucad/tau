`WaitIndicator` now keeps a shown indicator on screen for its 500 ms minimum counted from the frame it actually showed in, and times its CSS delays on the document timeline. Before, a long task could cut the minimum short twice over: a delay measured from `performance.now()` started on the timeline as it stood before the task, so it ended early by the task's length, and a reveal that a long task painted late still held only until its predicted time. `WaitClock` gains an optional `animationNow()`, the time a CSS animation started now counts its delay from (the default clock reads `document.timeline.currentTime`; clocks without it keep using `now()`), and `useWaitClock` returns `revealed`, to call from the reveal animation's `animationstart`. A wait that takes over an indicator whose reveal no frame has painted yet now counts its minimum from the frame that paints it, and the reveal's paint is read off the performance clock, since the first frame after a long task can carry a timeline time from before the task. A wait that ends after its reveal fell due but before any frame drew it now holds its minimum from the next frame, and shows nothing if that minimum, counted on the performance clock, would already be over or the page is hidden. Where the document runs no animations (jsdom) or the clock is injected, the predicted reveal stands. A takeover of words the server-rendered page revealed before hydration serves the minimum from its own frame, holding them longer, never shorter. A hold, and the label at the slow mark, now hide even when the frame that ends their 0.01 ms step lands a hair short of its end; before, the words could stay on screen for good.
